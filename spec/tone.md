# Tone

Controls that shape the instrument's character. Each is adjustable by the player, live.

## Strike

- Where the string is struck, controlling which harmonics are emphasised or suppressed.
- How hard and how soft the striking surface is, and how that changes with playing force.

## Harmonics

- Control over the audibility of individual harmonics or groups of harmonics.
- Control over how stretched or inharmonic the overtones are.
- Control over how quickly high and low harmonics fade relative to each other.

## String

- String material character, from the dense growl of a wound bass string to the bright ring of plain steel, as a continuous blend rather than a fixed choice.
- String weight and thickness, independent of pitch: heavier strings respond differently to the hammer, ring differently, and carry different overtones.
- Whether a change of string changes only the string's character, or also the touch and loudness, as it would on a real instrument.
- Number of strings per note (one, two, three, or in between), and how far apart they are tuned.

## Body

- How much the instrument body and other strings resonate along with what is played.

## Beyond the piano

- Settings that no real piano could have are welcome when they are musical.
